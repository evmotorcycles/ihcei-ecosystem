Cairn

Four small tools for checking things you were told.

No account, no internet connection, nothing sent anywhere. Everything below runs inside this page on your own device. Pick the thing you actually need.

What these tools cannot do None of them knows whether something is true. They check whether a claim is checkable, whether permission was actually granted, and whether a record has been altered. A sentence can pass every check here and still be wrong. Use them to see what you're missing — not as a verdict on reality.

Check a claim

Paste a statement someone gave you — from a person, an article, or an AI. You'll get back what can be checked in it, what's missing, and what to ask for next.

This is the permission list. Anything not written down here is refused — you don't have to think of every bad case in advance, only the good ones.

What this cannot do A permission list stops an app reaching things it was never allowed to reach. It does not stop an app misusing what you did allow, and it cannot tell whether the app is trustworthy. Grant the smallest thing that gets the job done.

    What this cannot do It makes tampering visible. It does not make tampering impossible, and it says nothing about whether an entry was true when it was written. Someone can still write a lie — they just can't rewrite it later without it showing.

    We failed our own test Engine confidence measures how checkable the SENTENCE is; ground truth measures how sound the PROJECT is. Different quantities, diverging by 0.37 on real data.

    What this means for you: Cairn confidence is NOT a probability the claim is true and NOT a quality score for the thing described. It measures the text.
