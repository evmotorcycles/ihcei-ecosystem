What this cannot do

    The order

    Three layers, and the middle one is where people get hurt

    Each layer sits inside the next. The reason this is on the screen and not in a manual: it is what stops a reading from being used for more than it can carry.

    Two things follow, and they are the whole reason HELM behaves the way it does.

    Saying nothing is a fact about the instrument, not about the message. When nothing here crosses the floor it means this instrument found nothing — not that the message was found safe. Empty is not the same as false.

    A reading cannot answer a bigger question than the layer it was taken at. Counting words is a reading at layer 1. “This person is lying” is a question at layer 3. HELM never crosses that gap, and it will tell you when something else has.

    How a decision gets made

    Six stages: four to set the order, two to reason inside it

    The first four settle what a thing is. Nothing is built and nothing is claimed. The last two are where you can be wrong on purpose — which is the point of them.

    Standard procedures

    One procedure per stage, and each one names what it turns away

    A procedure that refuses nothing is advice. Each of these ends in something you do, and something you decline to accept.

    Delegate

    What you have handed to machines

    One row per permission. Anything not on this list is refused by default — that is the rule, not a setting. Every row revokes in one tap and every decision lands in the wallet below.

    Develop

    Is this growing what you can do, or replacing it?

    Not a screen-time meter. After you use an assistant, say what happened. Only you ever see this.

    Prove

    What you checked, and when

    Each entry is sealed against the one before it. Change any past entry and the chain breaks at exactly that point — which is what makes it worth keeping.
