# The Governance container

Governance reads a field as a **communication system**: who encodes, who decodes, what
survives the channel, how fast errors are corrected, and what happens when they are not.
Its core quantities are **capacity `U`**, **two-hop fidelity `D = D_enc · D_dec`**,
**realized yield `E = U·D`**, and **enforcement latency `τ_v`**.

Reframing is a real and productive scientific move. Thermodynamics → information theory,
mechanics → variational principles, and computation → complexity classes were all
container changes, and each paid for itself in *new derivations*, not new vocabulary.
That is the bar.

## The crossover protocol

Four steps. **Step 4 is not optional** — a crossover that skips it is renaming, not
reframing.

1. **State the RT reading at full strength.** Invoke the `rational-thinking-framework`
   skill. Never transform a strawman.
2. **Identify the channel.** What is encoded, by what, decoded by what? Where is the
   information bottleneck? What plays the role of `U`, `D_enc`, `D_dec`, `E`, `τ_v`?
3. **Re-derive, do not re-label.** Recover a known result from the governance quantities.
   If nothing known can be recovered, the crossover has failed *at this stage* and must
   say so.
4. **Produce a discriminating prediction.** Name a measurement where the governance
   reading and the RT reading differ, or where the governance reading forbids something.
   If none exists, the reframe is **at most an interpretation** and must be labelled one.

## The three verdicts a crossover can earn

Every reframe must be assigned exactly one. Assign it explicitly.

| Verdict | Meaning | What it may claim |
|---|---|---|
| **Rival theory** | changes at least one prediction | must be tested; may be *wrong* |
| **Interpretation** | empirically equivalent; different story | coherence and parsimony only — **never** the RT field's predictive credit |
| **Category error** | contradicts a measurement or a no-go theorem | must be withdrawn or repaired |

**Most reframes are interpretations. Saying so is not a defeat** — it is the difference
between an honest reframe and an overclaim.

## Hard constraints the governance register must respect

A governance reading gains nothing by violating results that are proved, not assumed:

- **No-signalling.** Correlation is not communication. A framework that infers
  *zero information latency* from *perfect correlation* has contradicted a theorem.
  Shared correlation — however strong — transmits no bits.
- **Bell/device-independent certification is narrow.** It certifies specific
  cryptographic tasks under loophole-free conditions. It does not generalise to
  "the evaluator cannot be lied to" about arbitrary self-reports.
- **Decoherence is not observation.** Measurement statistics are recovered without any
  conscious observer. Any reading that makes rendering depend on a mind must confront
  this, not bypass it.
- **Metric claims are checkable.** If distance is asserted to be a function of latency,
  the induced metric must satisfy the metric axioms and its scaling exponent is a number
  that can come out wrong.

## The layer stack (inherited, non-negotiable)

- **`[L1]` measured / falsifiable** — the only layer that counts as evidence.
- **`[L2]` operational definitions** — the term→function mapping. Defensible, not a finding.
- **`[L3]` ontological prior** — "the universe is a rendered interface", "spacetime is a
  headset". These are **framing devices**. No `[L1]` result adjudicates them, and they must
  never be presented as conclusions of the empirical work.

Tag every claim. If asked to justify an `[L3]` claim with data, say plainly that no dataset
adjudicates it.

## Rules carried in from the empirical record

These were paid for in falsifications and they apply to any field, not just finance:

- **A test that cannot fail is not evidence.** Mark it `falsifiable: false` and exclude it
  from the score.
- **Anti-immunisation.** "The test failed, which shows the environment is broken, which
  proves the framework" is unfalsifiable. Refuse it — including when the user makes it.
- **Simulator rule.** A simulation that *generates* the structure a claim exploits cannot
  test that claim.
- **Ablation rule.** Any multi-component system must be tested by removing each component
  and measuring the loss. Name dead weight.
- **Refuted ≠ untestable-here ≠ blocked.** Keep the three distinct.
- **Newer and larger governs.** Never quote the friendlier sample.

## The honest summary of what the crossover has bought so far

Reframing scripture as governance produced **operational definitions that generate
falsifiable tests** — that is a genuine success of the method, independent of whether any
particular test passed. Reframing *finance* as governance produced **four falsifications
and one 4/5 result**, and the falsifications came from the method working.

Applied to physics, the crossover has so far produced **one `[L1]` result** (an emergent
metric from a pure coupling matrix, with a checkable scaling exponent) and **several
`[L3]` framings** that are interpretations at best — plus at least one **category error**
(the entanglement/zero-latency inference above). Report it that way.

### The quantum decoherence crossover, and the domain limit it bought

Spec `6cb42dcd`, **5/9**. The most instructive crossover run so far, because the target was
*exactly computable* rather than fitted. Two things came out of it, and both are worth
carrying forward:

- **Verdict: INTERPRETATION, declared before the run.** Identifying the channel — encode hop
  into the environment, decode hop into an observer's fragment — **is quantum Darwinism**.
  Zurek's redundancy already separates total system–environment correlation from
  fragment-limited access. The reframe changed no prediction of physics. Declaring the
  verdict in advance is what stopped a good numerical fit being read afterwards as a
  discovery. A reframe landing on an existing well-tested theory is a **validity check on
  the method** — the vocabulary mapped onto real structure instead of inventing it.

- **`E = U · D_enc · D_dec` now carries a stated domain limit.** Against the closed-form
  `I(S:F)`, the single-hop form `U·D_enc` errs by 0.0028 bits; the two-hop product errs by
  0.5495 — **roughly 195× worse.** The product form presumes the **decode hop is scarce**.
  Where the record is *redundantly replicated*, it is not, and the product form is simply the
  wrong shape. **Do not apply the product form to channels whose records are redundantly
  copied across the decoding substrate.** This does not touch yeast or GitHub, which are not
  redundant in that sense — it is a boundary, not a reversal.

**Method note worth keeping.** The pre-registration predicted the failure *and its
mechanism* correctly, and predicted its *location* wrongly (it lands at small fragment
fractions, not in the saturation region). Getting the direction right and the location wrong
is a partial miss and should be reported as one rather than rounded up to a successful
prediction.
