## The objection was right, and it was about the instrument

v1 printed the class name **"LABEL"**. That names a grammatical fact — the text names this
group with a noun rather than a finite verb — but *label* also names a **semantic register**:
a birthright or sectarian identity. One word for both invites exactly the conflation the
reading is trying to avoid. **The ambiguity was in the output vocabulary.**

Classes are now **VERBAL / MIXED / NOMINAL**. Renaming altered **no count v1 produced**, and
v1 is published unchanged and not re-scored.

### The register firewall — gate Y6 requires this verbatim in the output

> A NOMINAL verdict is **equally compatible** with a governance-state reading and with a
> birthright-identity reading, and **adjudicates neither**. Axis 1 measures how the text
> names a group. Neither reading is a morphological claim, so no morphological result can
> support or undermine either one.

## One correction to how v1 was described

X6 has been described as having **forced** the instrument to reject a symmetry assumption.
**It did not.** X6 is a **capability check** — can the screen return a non-verbal verdict at
all? The five non-verbal results came from the data. Had the data returned none, X6 would
have **failed**, and the correct report would have been *"this screen is broken"*, not
*"the framework is refuted"*. That is the difference between a control and a quota.

## The extension: a second axis

Morphology cannot reach the question the governance reading turns on: **can the state be
entered and left?** A birthright identity cannot; a state-variable can. Measured by
`بَعْدَ` + a possessed state-noun, and by explicit reversion verbs — with its own negative
control, because one does not become or cease to be Thamūd.

## A limit found after the run

The axis-2 construction needs a **possessed state-noun**. For five terms — *taqwā-hum,
hūd-hum, shirk-hum, nifāq-hum, ṣabr-hum* — that form **does not occur in the text at all**.
Their 0 means **the measurement could not fire**, not that no mutability exists.

Axis 2 is interpretable only for **islām, īmān, naṣr**. **Reporting *munāfiqūn* as FIXED
would overclaim**, so it isn't.

## The answer is a measurement, not an agreement

- **īmān — 8.** `كَفَرُوا۟ بَعْدَ إِيمَـٰنِهِمْ` (3:86, 3:90) and
  `ءَامَنُوا۟ ثُمَّ كَفَرُوا۟ ثُمَّ ءَامَنُوا۟ ثُمَّ كَفَرُوا۟` (4:137). The entry-and-exit
  pattern a state-variable reading predicts.
- **islām — 1.** `وَكَفَرُوا۟ بَعْدَ إِسْلَـٰمِهِمْ` (9:74) — *"they disbelieved after their
  islām."* **Real, and incompatible with a pure birthright reading** — but one ayah against
  īmān's eight, and below the bar of 2 fixed before scoring.
- **munāfiqūn — the measurement could not fire.** Nothing claimed either way.

**And a MUTABLE verdict still does not establish a governance state-variable.** It
establishes only that the text describes movement in or out of whatever the term denotes.
