Do not argue with the peel

A pomegranate is peel, bitter pith and seeds around the part you want. You do not argue with the peel — you press it, and what runs out is what you can use. This does the same with a sentence: it presses out the things you could go and check, and tells you which one to do first.

Nothing leaves this device. What you paste is not sent anywhere, and there is nothing here that could send it.

Why a vague sentence scores nothing

“Experts generally agree that our meters are highly accurate.” That sounds professional and careful, and press it and nothing comes out — there is no source to open, no figure to look up, no date to compare. This cannot be shown false. That is not the same as false; it is worse. Wording like it survives every argument, because there is nothing in it to lose.

The specific version — a named body, a year, a percentage, a stated way of measuring — can be destroyed in one phone call. That is why it reads high here even when it is invented. Pressing a well-made lie traps it inside its own structure.

When many handles read low, that is the warning, not the comfort. With everything hanging off one source, five handles are not five ways in. They are one way in, counted five times, and each reads one twenty-fifth because the arithmetic is saying they are not independent.
