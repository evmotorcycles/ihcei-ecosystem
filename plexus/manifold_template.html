What the exponent tells you With one route between you and a thing, its resistance is 1/J and the metric is d = J−1/2 — exactly, at every J. So an exponent of −0.500 means there is exactly one way in, and losing it strands the thing entirely. Why that is not a general law Add a second route and raising the direct coupling buys far less collapse, because the other route was already holding the two ends close. Measured here: −0.019 at J = 0.02 with a second route, against −0.500 without one. The exponent climbs back toward −½ only once the direct link dominates everything else. It is a reading of redundancy, not a constant. Idle is not gone Something you are not using keeps a small coupling rather than none. At zero it would have no route at all, the metric would rightly refuse to place it, and it would vanish instead of drifting to the edge. What this is not It is not an operating system. Nothing here installs a real program, runs a real model, reads your files, or leaves the browser. It is a sandbox in which the same tested arithmetic that reads a bill also arranges a workspace, so the idea can be measured instead of described. Nothing leaves this device No account, no network, no server. What you register is kept in this browser only.
