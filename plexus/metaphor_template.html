Plexus Is this picture a lens or a covering?
Back

Newton's billiard balls could be killed. That is what made them useful.

He imagined light as tiny hard particles. The picture predicted the angle off a mirror, the bend into glass, and a perfectly sharp shadow. Two of those came back false — light travels slower in water, not faster, and it does bend round an edge. The picture died of its own predictions.

So there is one question to ask of any picture: what does it predict that could come back false, and who is able to make that prediction come true?

Nothing leaves this device.

What this is not telling you

  • This measures pictures, not software.
  • Every prediction list here was written by a person.
  • Self-referring is not an accusation of bad faith, and every working demonstration is one.

Then go and check

  • Take a picture you were shown and write down one thing it predicts that could come back false.
  • Then ask whether the people who showed it to you could make that come true by changing their own work.
How to overturn any verdict here

The classing and the arithmetic are not hand-written, but the readings are. If you can state a prediction that has been missed, add one and the class changes. That is the whole way to overturn this page.

Nobody could have rescued Newton's particles by editing their own work — only the theory could give way. A picture whose every prediction can be made true again by the people showing it to you is answering to them, not to the world.

A tool built on a self-referring picture can be excellent, and a tool built on a lens can be useless. Nothing here says whether anything ships, sells or helps anybody.
