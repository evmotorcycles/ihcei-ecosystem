The stop card

Refusing to guess is one of two normal outcomes, not a failure. This is what that has to look like on a phone so nobody reads it as a crash.

Check a claim

The trap is in the name

Design to an empty tray and you will build absence: dead space, a centred line, a spinner that stopped. That is exactly what a crash looks like, so a person reads the more familiar of the two. The tray must not be empty. What comes back is a returned parcel with a slip on it — you get your thing back, plus a note saying what was missing. Nobody has ever thought the postal service crashed.

So the signal that separates a stop from a crash is not the absence of an answer. It is the presence of the work that led to not giving one.

Eleven rules the card follows

  1. Fill the card.Crash states are centred in dead space. A stop is left-aligned and full, like a document.
  2. Show counts.“Read 12 words · looked for 5 kinds of support · found 1.” A broken app cannot produce a number.
  3. Same silhouette as an answer.Strip, headline, counts, reasons, next step, footer — identical in both outcomes, so the shape says “normal” before any word is read.
  4. Stamp ink, not a traffic light.No red: this is not an error. No green tick either: the person’s question did not get answered. A third colour that means held.
  5. First person, past tense.“I stopped before guessing.” A completed action reads as done. “No results” is ambiguous between searched-and-found-none and search-broke.
  6. Name what was avoided.The headline says the system could have guessed and chose not to. That is the protection, stated.
  7. A timestamp and a fingerprint.The strongest anti-crash evidence available: a crash cannot produce a hash of its own output.
  8. Point the next step at the input.“Add a source” fixes the claim. “Try again” fixes the app — and would be a lie, since the same words honestly produce the same answer.
  9. Never clear the input.Words vanishing while a panel appears is the most crash-like sensation an interface has.
  10. Let it settle, don’t let it flash.A short damped arrival reads as a process completing. Things disappearing reads as failure. Off under reduced-motion.
  11. Say up front there are two outcomes.Then a stop is recognised rather than discovered. It is the interface version of pre-registering.

What this page cannot do The checker behind it does not understand language. It counts five kinds of support — a named source, a specific figure, how it was measured, a date, and who it applies to — and pattern-matches ambiguity. A well-written falsehood carrying all five passes, and a true sentence carrying none is stopped. The design question on this page is how to present a stop, and that is answered independently of how good the checker is.
