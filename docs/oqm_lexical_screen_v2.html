Two axes, and a firewall around one word

The objection was right, and it was an objection to my instrument: v1 printed the class name "LABEL", which names a grammatical fact but also names a semantic register. One word for both invites exactly the conflation the reading is trying to avoid.

The register firewall — gate Y6 requires this verbatim in the output

A NOMINAL verdict is equally compatible with a governance-state reading and with a birthright-identity reading, and adjudicates neither. Axis 1 measures how the text names a group. Neither reading is a morphological claim, so no morphological result can support or undermine either one.

One correction to how v1 was described

X6 has been described as having forced the instrument to reject a symmetry assumption. It did not.

X6 is a capability check: can the screen return a non-verbal verdict at all? The five non-verbal results came from the data. Had the data returned none, X6 would have failed, and the correct report would have been "this screen is broken" — not "the framework is refuted".

A gate that manufactured its own answer would be worthless. That distinction is the difference between a control and a quota.

The actual extension: a second axis

Morphology cannot reach the question the governance reading turns on.

Axis 1

How is the group named?

By a finite verb — "those who [verb]" — or only by a noun? A proper noun cannot be conjugated to name its own bearers.

Axis 2 — new

Can the state be entered and left?

A birthright identity cannot be entered or exited. A state-variable can. Measured by بَعْدَ + a possessed state-noun, and by explicit reversion verbs.

A limit found after the run

Seven of ten "can't fire" — and that is not the same as "fixed"

The primary axis-2 construction needs a possessed state-noun. For five terms — taqwā-hum, hūd-hum, shirk-hum, nifāq-hum, ṣabr-hum — that form does not occur in the text at all. Their score of 0 means the measurement could not fire, not that no mutability exists.

Axis 2 is interpretable only for the three whose state-noun is attested: islām, īmān, naṣr. Reporting munāfiqūn as FIXED would overclaim, so it isn't reported that way.

Found after the run, and written into the runner's own disclosures rather than smoothed over.

So the answer is a measurement, not an agreement

Where axis 2 can fire:

īmān — 8. The text repeatedly describes entering and leaving it: كَفَرُوا۟ بَعْدَ إِيمَـٰنِهِمْ (3:86, 3:90), and ءَامَنُوا۟ ثُمَّ كَفَرُوا۟ ثُمَّ ءَامَنُوا۟ ثُمَّ كَفَرُوا۟ (4:137). That is the entry-and-exit pattern a state-variable reading predicts.

islām — 1. The single attestation is 9:74: وَكَفَرُوا۟ بَعْدَ إِسْلَـٰمِهِمْ — "and they disbelieved after their islām." Real, and incompatible with a pure birthright reading — but one ayah against īmān's eight, and below the bar of 2 fixed before scoring.

munāfiqūn — the measurement could not fire. Nothing is claimed either way.

And a MUTABLE verdict still does not establish a governance state-variable

It establishes only that the text describes movement in or out of whatever the term denotes. That is a smaller fact, and it is the whole of what was measured.

Both axes carry their own negative control, both boundaries were fixed before any term was scored, and v1 is published unchanged — renaming its classes altered no count it produced. Neither axis establishes meaning; neither is a statement about any living community.
