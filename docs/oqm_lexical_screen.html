An instrument that can say no

A methodology gets more comprehensive by acquiring ways to settle questions, not by accumulating answers to them. So this isn't another reading — it's one screen, applied to a whole vocabulary at once, calibrated at both ends, and required by its own gate to be capable of disagreeing with the framework that built it.

It disagrees with the framework, on purpose

Five of ten terms come back as LABEL

Including muslim and munāfiqūn — terms the framework reads as functions. The verb aslama plainly exists and is used, but this text names a group by that act once. Munāfiqūn: twice.

A gate was written specifically to catch a screen that certifies everything its author hoped for. X6 required at least two LABELs and got five. Had it got zero, the correct verdict would have been "this screen is broken", not "the framework is confirmed".

And two come back strongly ACTION

268 designations for āmanū. If the reading is that membership is something you do rather than something you are, this text puts that weight on believing — not on submitting, not on the group-nouns.

The gate that failed gave the screen a scope condition

ṣābiʾīn cannot be screened at all. Its root supplies no attested finite verb anywhere in the text — so the instrument has nothing to look for.

That is untestable-here, not label, and the difference matters. Naṣārā and ḥawāriyyūn score zero despite their roots having plenty of verbs — for naṣārā, 82 finite tokens. Filing "the text never uses the available verb for this group" together with "there is no verb to use" would merge two different findings.

So the screen now carries a stated precondition: the root must supply an attested finite verb. X1 failed, and that failure is what produced the condition.

Three defects, all caught by the calibrators

Not one of them by inspection. This is what the controls are for.

  1. The clitic stripper ate a first radical. كفروا begins with kāf, which is also the "like/as" proclitic — so the stripper turned kafarū into farū. The commonest verbal designation in the text scored 3 instead of 171. The positive control caught it.
  2. The ḥawāriyyūn forms were wrong — they missed the attested يُحَاوِرُهُۥ. The integrity gate caught it.
  3. "And they did" is one token. وَعَمِلُوا۟ carries the conjunction inside the word, so ʿamilū scored 8 instead of 58. Fixed with a uniform proclitic rule applied to every term and every control — not a patch to one word list.

Each fix changed the instrument. None changed a threshold. The boundary set before scoring stands where it was.

What an ACTION classification does not buy

It does not vindicate a reading. It establishes that the text uses a verbal group-designation — a far smaller fact than an interpretation, and the two should never be traded for one another.

It says nothing about meaning. Many perfectly ordinary group-names are historically built on verbs, in every language.

And nothing about any living community — Jewish, Christian, Muslim, Zoroastrian or otherwise. The units of analysis are Arabic word-forms in one text, and that is where the result stops.

What this contributes: not a claim, a screen — reusable, calibrated at both ends, with a boundary fixed in advance, a stated precondition, and a gate that makes disagreement possible. Anyone can point it at a new term tomorrow and get an answer that isn't guaranteed to flatter them.
