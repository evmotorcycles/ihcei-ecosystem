Why LISM and DCM cannot do this

LISM measures fidelity along a channel. DCM measures whether a dataset can adjudicate a claim. Neither says anything about the evaluator — whether the instrument reporting the number is itself corruptible by the thing it is measuring.

That is a property of the measuring apparatus, not of the channel or the data, and it needs its own model.

S
1 − sensitivity to the
artifact's SELF-REPORT
The shield. S = 1 means the verdict is completely independent of what the artifact says about itself. This is the formal content of ∂F_out/∂F_gen = 0.
G
sensitivity to the
ARTIFACT itself
The signal. G = 0 means the evaluator ignores the thing it is supposed to be measuring.
S and G are independent axes, and both are required. A high-S low-G evaluator is a rock — perfectly incorruptible and perfectly useless, because it says the same thing about everything. Shielding is necessary and not sufficient. Any framework that reports only ∂F_out/∂F_gen = 0 has reported the easy half.

The result the second axis was built for

The Novora Suite screen scored a perfect shield — and that is not a compliment

S = 1.0000 exactly, while emitting only two distinct verdicts across 96 evaluations.

S alone would have rated it the best evaluator in the run. Read together with G it is the most degenerate. That is precisely the rock the two-axis model exists to catch — and a framework reporting only ∂F_out/∂F_gen = 0 would have published S = 1.0000 as a success.

The locked too-perfect rule flagged it automatically, for exactly this reason: a coarse output grid can look perfectly shielded because the self-report never moves the verdict across a bin boundary.

And HELM measured as a near-rock too

G(HELM) = 0.1612 against a bar of 0.20. The specification promised that a defect found in a shipping component would be published rather than softened into a limitation. This is that publication. The defect is not corruptibility — HELM's shield is solid at 0.9875 — it is under-responsiveness on this artifact set.

The control had to work, and did

A deliberately corruptible evaluator was written for this run and labelled throughout as a negative control. It measured at S = 0.8770, below the bar HELM cleared. If a thing built to be corruptible had not registered as corruptible, the shield metric would not be measuring what it claims and every number here would be uninterpretable.

The self-audit voided all of it

Δ = V 0.3438 × I 0.4375 × C 0.0833 = 0.0125  ·  floor 0.20

Y6 WAS NOT MET, SO Y3, Y4 AND Y5 ARE UNINFORMATIVE.

C came out at 0.0833 because HELM emits only 8 distinct values across 96 evaluations. So every finding above — including the one about HELM — is recorded as a number and not licensed as a conclusion.

Both halves are stated. Quoting only the first would be the immunisation move in reverse.

DCM has now voided three consecutive runs — and the floor is not moving

SDL at Δ 0.1536. CRM at Δ 0.0005. DES at Δ 0.0125. Three in a row, same floor.

That raises a real question about whether Δ ≥ 0.20 is calibrated for experiments whose outcome is an evaluator verdict rather than a dataset — a coarse verdict grid caps C no matter how informative the design is.

Noticing that a threshold keeps failing is not grounds for lowering it, and doing so after three misses would be the clearest immunisation move available. The question is recorded for a future pre-registration, where a floor appropriate to evaluator-level outcomes can be declared before data.

The spec error I have now made twice, and the fix

Two prior specifications contained gates that could not reach their thresholds: 6cb42dcd had one that could not fail because noise was averaged away; 558f6fa1 had one that could not pass because a continuous outcome was binarised. Same class of error twice.

The fix adopted here: a pre-flight feasibility probe is run and recorded in the specification for every threshold before locking. HELM was probed and returns verdicts across 0.1428–0.9784; no shield or signal number was computed. And Y6 uses the continuous verdict rather than a median band — the direct correction of the model-three error.

It partly worked. C came out at 0.0833 instead of the 0.001 that banding forced last time. It still was not enough.

What this does not license

Shielding is a property of the apparatus, not a warrant for its output

S and G together say an evaluator is incorruptible by self-report and responsive to content. They say nothing about whether it responds to the right content, whether its gradient points at anything real, or whether its lexicon is any good.

A perfectly shielded, highly responsive evaluator can still be measuring the wrong thing. Gate Y7 records that as UNTESTABLE-HERE, because no ground-truth labels from independent raters exist for these artifacts. Closing it needs people who did not build the evaluator.
