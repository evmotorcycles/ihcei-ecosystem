The VIF number does not survive its own null

A VIF of 1.000073 was offered as proof that the text handles seeking and sharing as independent channel legs. I ran it — and then I ran 1,200 pairs of unrelated random words through the identical test.

VIF for 1,200 frequency-matched pairs of random, unrelated words across 6,236 ayahs. Nearly the entire distribution sits against the wall at 1.0.

VIF 1.0001.05 — the "orthogonal" bar1.10+

99.8% of random unrelated pairs clear the orthogonality bar. The null median is 1.000032 — the same order as the number being cited as a finding.

Where the measured value actually lands

Not at the edge of the distribution. In the middle of it.

VIF(SALAT, ZAKAT) 1.000028 r = −0.005302 null median 1.000032 its percentile 46th → 54% of arbitrary unrelated word pairs are MORE "orthogonal" than salat and zakat.

The arithmetic is not mysterious. VIF = 1/(1−r²), and across 6,236 ayahs almost every word is absent from almost every ayah. Two rare tracks are both nearly all zeros, so r lands near zero whatever the words mean. Near-1.0 is the floor the substrate forces, not a property anyone designed.

A test that cannot fail is not evidence

Before a near-1.0 VIF can be cited, someone has to show a near-1.0 VIF wasn't guaranteed. On this substrate it was. The quoted 1.000073 supports no claim about how the text handles anything.

28 of 28 clear the bar. A metric that reports īmān and muʾmin — built on the same three letters — as independent is not measuring independence on this data. It is measuring how often two rare words miss each other.

And it is not the same quantity as the yeast number

The 1.0026 on yeast and 1.0203 on GitHub were computed on continuous per-node features — clustering coefficient against betweenness centrality; commit-message consistency against outsider-merge rate. Every unit has a real value on both axes, so collinearity is a live possibility and a low VIF is informative.

Per-ayah word counts are overwhelmingly zero. Sharing the name "VIF" does not make the two numbers comparable, and printing them in the same table implies a comparability that isn't there.

The other gate failed too, and that one is a real finding

Using the same designation matcher that scored 7 of 7 control proper nouns at zero.

"those who believed" ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ root A-M-N 268 times "those who submitted" ٱلَّذِينَ أَسْلَمُوا۟ root S-L-M 1 time (5:44)

The gate required at least 5 for each root. It got 268 and 1, so it is reported failed.

What that actually shows

The verb aslama plainly exists and is used — roughly 22 finite tokens. But this text names a group by that act exactly once.

The group-by-action designation attaches overwhelmingly to īmān, not to islām. If the reading is that membership is something you do rather than something you are, the text puts that weight on believing, not on submitting — 268 to 1.

That is a genuine distributional fact, produced by a test already shown to discriminate. The VIF is not.

The integrity gate earned its place

The first run failed W1. Three track patterns matched nothing, because hamza-on-waw (U+0624) decomposes under Unicode NFD into a plain waw plus a combining mark — so muʾmin is written مومن after normalisation, not مءمن. And zakāt carries a waw: زكوٰة.

The regexes were corrected and the run repeated. W2's result is unchanged by that fix, because the null never depended on the track definitions — which is why the headline finding survives a bug that broke three of eight tracks.

What none of this licenses

Nothing about what any word means. Counting is not interpreting.

Nothing about any living community — Jewish, Christian or Muslim. The units of analysis are Arabic word-forms in one text.

And nothing about design. Even if two word-tracks had come out independent, distributional independence is not evidence of authorial architecture. That inference does not follow, and it would not have followed if the number had gone the other way.
