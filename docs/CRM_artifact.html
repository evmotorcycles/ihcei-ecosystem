CRM — the Cognitive Reference ModelFidelity is undefined until you name what it is fidelity to. And on this run, that idea did not earn its keep.

LISM asks how much signal survives a channel. CRM asks faithful to what? — and staked its own existence on two references beating one. It missed by 0.002.

Why LISM could not do this alone

LISM carries a single fidelity term. That is not an oversight — it is reference-neutral by construction, measuring how much signal survives a channel rather than what the signal is supposed to be faithful to.

In a perceptual system that is exactly the missing degree of freedom. An agent can be highly faithful to payoff while being unfaithful to the world, and LISM has nowhere to write that down. So CRM splits the term in two.

DW
|rank corr( g(x), x )|
Fidelity to the world. Does the percept preserve the ordering of the actual state?
DF
|rank corr( g(x), f(x) )|
Fidelity to the payoff. Does the percept preserve the ordering of what the state is worth?

Both are computed from the perceptual map and the world only. Neither reads the agent's realised payoff — a test scans the source of both functions to enforce it.

X5 failed — and the spec had already written down what that means

CRM beat LISM's own two-hop form by 0.00304 against a locked bar of 0.005. The pre-registration says, in its own words:

“If LISM's existing form predicts this cognitive outcome as well as a purpose-built two-reference model, then CRM adds vocabulary and no power, and the honest conclusion is to keep LISM and drop CRM.”

And there is a harsher number that was not pre-registered, reported because leaving it out would flatter the model. DF alone scores 0.06641 against the two-reference model's 0.06611 — a gain of 0.0003. The locked comparison used the mean of the two fidelities, which is a weaker rival than the better single one. Against the best single reference, two references buy almost nothing.

The one thing that did look like a discovery

The discriminating prediction was a sign flip: as the payoff becomes monotone in the world state, the correlation between the two fidelities should swing from negative to positive. The monotonicity knob was swept, not fitted.

m = 0.75 +0.957
m = 1.00 +1.000
correlation between DW and DF · m is the payoff-monotonicity knob

It passed, and it trips the too-perfect rule

Two values exceed the locked 0.95 flag: 0.957 and exactly 1.000. The reading is not leakage but degeneracy — when payoff is strictly increasing in the world state, the two references are the same ordering, so the correlation is forced to 1.

That is why half (b) of this gate was disclosed as near-definitional before the run. Only the negative correlation at m = 0.00 carries information.

I mis-specified the self-audit — and the binding fires anyway

The spec routed CRM through DCM's self-audit, with power to void the whole run. It said to band realised payoff at its median. But DCM's C factor is distinct outcome values ÷ n — so binarising a continuous outcome forces C = 2/2000 = 0.001 by construction. The gate could not pass whatever the data did.

Δ = V 0.5000 × I 0.9600 × C 0.0010 = 0.0005  ·  floor 0.20

Why the gate is not being re-scored

On the unbanded payoff C would be 1.0000 — 2,000 distinct values across 2,000 agents. That number is a diagnosis of my error, not a substitute verdict.

The binding is honoured in full. Honouring a binding rule only when it is convenient is precisely the immunisation move the rule exists to stop. So every result above, including the one that looks like a discovery, is reported as uninformative.

Second consecutive self-inflicted un-passable gate

In the previous run the cognitive arm could not fail because noise was averaged away. Here the self-audit could not pass because the outcome was binarised. Different errors, same class: a threshold written without checking the quantity could reach it. That is a pattern about this programme's spec-writing, and it is recorded as one rather than as two unlucky accidents.

The fix did work where it was aimed: X2 — the populated-failing-region gate added specifically to catch the earlier error — passed, with payoff spread 0.1815 against a floor of 0.02.

Only two of seven tools could change a verdict, and both of them ruled against the new model. A governance verdict on prose is not evidence about agents. Saying so in the specification, in advance, is what stops a large toolchain looking like it was doing scientific work it was not doing.

What this says about people

Nothing. And that is not a figure of speech.

Four Hugging Face searches for human behavioural trial data — memory recall, judgment, calibration, decision trials — returned only text and multiple-choice QA corpora. No human trial dataset was reachable. Every agent here is simulated.

The substrate is a published evolutionary-game setting, which makes the world and the payoffs principled — but it is still a simulation, not a derivation. A simulation can show a decomposition is useful inside a model and say nothing at all about whether it is true of minds.

Where that leaves model three

CRM did not earn its existence on this run. It is better than LISM on this task — but by 0.003 against a bar of 0.005, and by 0.0003 against the best single reference. The spec said what to do in that case and the answer was: keep LISM.

And by the binding rule, I am not even entitled to that conclusion. The self-audit voided the run. The honest status of CRM is untested, not refuted — which means the next step is not to defend it but to re-register it, with a self-audit that can actually pass and a rival comparison against the best single reference rather than the mean.

A model that stakes its existence on a threshold, misses it, and says so is worth more than one that never risked anything. Model three does not get to join the stack on this evidence. That is the mechanism working, not the mechanism failing.
