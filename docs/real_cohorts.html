What was wrongA seeded simulation is not evidence

The cohort audit had flagged exactly two LISM cohorts as simulation, not evidence: the N=793 "knowledge cohort" (its own provenance says synthetic:true, seed 20260720) and the 500-node digital swarm (seed 20260719).

A seeded simulation that reproduces itself is a code-correctness check. It cannot be repaired by re-running it, and no number of green ticks converts it into support for a law about the world. The only honest remedy is the one already owed to the lost GitHub 992 cohort: pre-register the gates, fetch real data, commit the rows, and report whatever comes out.

MethodThe lock came before the data

The pre-registration was written, canonicalised and SHA-256 locked (4e83893b…), then committed in its own commit before the fetcher existed. Git history — not a promise — is the evidence that the gates preceded the data. The fetcher computes no gate and prints no verdict, so there is nothing in it to tune toward a result.

Three crawls were run, all before a single gate was computed. The first seed list reached only 69 nodes, under the pre-registered floor of 250; a node cap then truncated the graph at depth 1, under the floor of 3. Both were fixed blind to every outcome — only the sample's size and reach moved, never a threshold. Attempt one is preserved on disk with its disclosure, and a test asserts it is still there.

That is the difference between changing a sample and shopping for one. It only counts because the ordering is checkable by someone who doesn't trust me.

Bigger projects do buy fidelity. The gate required ρ ≤ 0.50.

spearman(U, D) = +0.5695

This is the mechanism behind KR1, and the answer to why knowledge exchange failed: if D is substantially redundant with U, multiplying them adds noise rather than information. The thesis assumed the two were separable. On real data they are not.

The bank · prereg 00d5d277 · N=992Designed prescriptively, tested until it broke

The N=44 failure prompted a reframe: raw capacity carries momentum in an unguided environment, so a descriptive survival contest rewards status over protocol work. The answer proposed was a prescriptive design — impose a fidelity floor D ≥ D_min, hold full reserves, deploy equity rather than debt.

That reframe is coherent — and one step from being unfalsifiable. "The test failed, which proves the environment is broken, which validates our design" immunises a theory against all future evidence. The pre-registration names that hazard in its own text and refuses it, by converting the claim into a prediction that can come out wrong:

P1. If capacity momentum is what masks fidelity, then within strata of similar capacity the fidelity signal must reappear. If it doesn't, the masking explanation is simply false.

P1 — fidelity survives stratification Failed

The make-or-break gate. It failed — but read which clause.

weighted within-stratum AUC = 0.7487 (threshold 0.55) ✓ cleared pooled, unstratified AUC = 0.7462 → stratifying did NOT destroy the signal usable strata = 2 of 5 (needed 3) ✗ FAILED HERE

Three of the five star strata are 100% default, so AUC is undefined there — unforeseeable at lock time. In the two strata where discrimination is measurable, τ_v scores 0.73 and 0.77. The threshold was not moved; the gate is recorded as failed with the diagnosis beside it.

P3 — a better tail, not a better mean Failed badly
conventional book (top stars) mean 46.4% 95th pct 50.3% sovereign book (floor + low τ_v) mean 87.5% 95th pct 90.1%

Not marginally worse — nearly twice the default rate. The earlier design predicted a lower mean and lost that at N=44; this gate tested the downside instead, and lost that too.

It met its gate, and that is recorded. But a 0.5-point margin with 46 nodes in one arm is not support. A test asserts the margin stays under 2 points so it can never be cited as a result. P5, the full-reserve invariant, holds by construction and is marked falsifiable: false — excluded from the score entirely.

Why it failed — the mechanism, which is the actual finding

The fidelity floor anti-selects. Splitting the cohort on it:

clears the floor (D ≥ D_min)93589.9%

 Capacity overwhelmingly drives survival here — the top star quintile defaults at 2.5% against a 75.6% base rate. So the admission rule systematically buys the failing half of the population. The book's tail is worse because the floor works exactly as specified.

The deepest problem this exposed

On the real PyPI graph, ρ(capacity, D) = +0.5695. On the real GitHub cohort, −0.4702. Two real, committed substrates disagree on the sign. D is therefore not yet measuring a stable construct, and every claim resting on "fidelity" inherits that instability.

This is a measurement problem, to be fixed by re-deriving D — never by reinterpreting a gate. A test keeps the disagreement visible so it cannot quietly drop out of the record.
