Every element is a node in a graph of what depends on what. The distance between two of them is the electrical resistance between them on that graph, and the picture is whatever best preserves those distances. Move a dependency and the layout rearranges because the arithmetic changed.

The −0.5 slope is arithmetic, not a discovery

Scaling every wire by the same J contracts every distance by J−1/2 — exactly, with an R² of 1.000000. That is not a property of this graph or any graph: pinv(J·L) = J⁻¹·pinv(L), so it holds on a ring, a path, a star, a dense random graph, and anything else you pass it.

The sweep is kept because it is a good smoke test — it fails the moment the pseudo-inverse, the Laplacian or the clipping breaks. It is reported as IDENTITY (CONTROL), never as a pass, and never as evidence that space is emergent.

Two things the specification got wrong

Broken links do not give infinite distance

Cut a mesh in two and the pseudo-inverse still returns a modest finite number between the halves — 1.118 here, smaller than a real distance inside one half. Unguarded, the layout would place two unrelated elements next to each other. Components are detected explicitly, and the engine returns infinity itself.

Zero coupling gives zero distance, not infinity

With every wire cut, the Laplacian is zero, its pseudo-inverse is zero, and every distance is 0.0. A completely dead mesh measures as perfectly contracted — visually identical to a perfectly coupled one. The dead case is caught before the metric is trusted.

Rendered by the real engine, offline, on this machine. No model, no weights, no network, no cloud. The whole layout for a hundred elements is one pseudo-inverse.
